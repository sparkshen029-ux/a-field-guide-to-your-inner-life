**Try this before you read on.** Close your eyes for ten seconds. Imagine walking down a street you've never seen. There's a door you've never noticed. Behind it, a house. Don't try to make it nice — just notice what showed up. Cracked walls? A room you'd rather not open? Whatever you just saw is what entry 14 works with, and this guide is about why that house deserves a longer look. ([Part IV](guide/04-the-house-and-the-people-inside.md))

---

Most guides to life cover the outer half: how not to die early, how not to lose money, how not to sign away your freedom. Good ones exist. [HowToLiveBetter](https://github.com/eternity4719/HowToLiveBetter) is the best of them, and this guide is its companion on the other side.

The other half is the one you lie awake with at 3 a.m.: the moods that arrive uninvited, the dream that keeps coming back, the voice that criticizes you on schedule, the tightness in your chest on Sunday evening. This guide covers that half.

**Where it comes from.** The tradition it draws on is **imagery dialogue** (*yixiang duìhuà*), a working Chinese school of psychology founded by Zhu Jianjun, which treats dreams and mental images as the mind's native language. Zhu's main books have never been translated into English. This guide is an exposition, not a translation and not his voice: it carries a selection of the tradition's most useful ideas into plain English, checks them against Western research where the two can be compared, and labels every claim so you know what you are standing on.

## How to read it

Every entry ends with an **Honesty note** in three layers:

- ***The tradition*** — what the imagery-dialogue school teaches, which you can trace to its books;
- ***The research*** — what empirical work supports, cited only when a source exists;
- ***My own experience*** — one practitioner's report, labeled as such and never dressed up as the other two.

The guide makes three promises. It will not pass tradition off as science. It will not pretend one book is therapy. And it will not pretend its author follows all of it — he has done about a tenth, and he wrote the thing.

## The AI skill

Install [`skills/inner-life-guide`](skills/inner-life-guide/README.md) into Claude Code or Codex and ask things like *"why do I keep dreaming about the same exam"* or *"what do I do with this anger"*. The skill looks up the relevant entries first, answers in the guide's three-layer style, cites the entry number, and says so when the guide has nothing on your question. In a crisis it hands you the hotlines before anything else.

## A note on scope

This guide does not diagnose, treat, or replace a professional relationship. It also does not do fortune-telling: entry 13 explains what a dream cannot tell you, and why. If you are in crisis, go directly to [entry 24](guide/06-the-edge-of-self-help.md#24-if-you-are-thinking-of-ending-your-life).
