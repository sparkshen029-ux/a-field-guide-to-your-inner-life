### 23. When this book is not enough

There is a line past which a guide, any guide, this one especially, becomes the wrong tool, and you deserve to know where I draw it.

Consider grief, because it shows the line at its clearest. Entry 5 said most people carry most losses, and that is the evidence and I stand by it. But there is a recognized minority condition where grief does not begin its slow work: months pass, half a year and more, and the person remains stuck at full intensity; eating, sleeping, working, parenting all stay broken; life itself can come to feel like the thing that ended. Roughly one in ten grieving adults land there, and it is not weakness, and it does not respond to tips. What it does respond to, on good evidence, is specific, structured therapy: in one landmark trial, targeted treatment helped roughly half the people who received it, against about a quarter on ordinary supportive counseling, and faster. That is a *good* statistic. It is the statistics of a treatable condition.

The general form of the line, then. This book's territory is the ordinary inner weather of an ordinary life: moods, dreams, images, self-relationship. Beyond the line are: weeks of losing the ability to feel pleasure in anything; sleep that stays broken; fear that has annexed whole neighborhoods of your life; any turn toward self-destruction; and, most relevant to a book about dreams, any sign that the practical business of living is being carried by someone who is no longer really there. Those are medical matters with effective treatments, and using a guide for them is like using a field guide to set a bone.

One more sentence, because cultural noise makes it necessary: seeking that help is not a Western thing, a weakness thing, or a confession thing. It is the same move as entry 6, made with the body's own judgment: something is being guarded, and it needs backup. Getting backup is the skill. Every other entry in this book assumes you have it.

> **Honesty note.** *The tradition:* this school draws the same line; imagery dialogue is a clinical method in China and its teachers are explicit that it is not for everyone without a clinician. *The research:* prevalence and treatment of prolonged grief disorder (Lundorff and colleagues; Shear's trial, via *HowToLiveBetter* §29 #8–9 with sources). *My own experience:* the bravest thing most of my students ever did was not an image exercise. It was a phone call to a hospital, holding a name they'd been afraid to say out loud.

### 24. If you are thinking of ending your life

If the thought of ending your life has become a visitor, a quiet one, or a loud one, this entry is the whole book for you tonight, and the rest can wait.

Do two things, in this order.

First: put the thought in front of someone with a pulse. Not a book; a person, trained, awake, paid or happily unpaid to sit with exactly this. There is a crisis line for your region, and this is what they are for; you do not need to be "sick enough," you do not need a plan, and you are not taking anyone's slot by calling. The international directory at findahelpline.com lists verified lines country by country; if you are in the United States, call or text 988; in the United Kingdom and Ireland, Samaritans, 116 123; in mainland China, 12356. Numbers change and lists age; if a number here fails, the directory will not.

Second: borrow the book's own logic and treat the thought itself as the entry-6 figure it is. The thought *sounds* like clear sight: it claims to be the only honest assessment of a hopeless file. It is not a sighting; it is a state. This particular guard reports only one thing, forever, and it reports it loudest at the hours when minds are most tired and least trustworthy (entry 22). Its data gets no better at 3 a.m. You do not have to believe the thought to survive it. You only have to not act on it while it is the loudest voice in the room. Hand the night to a person, and revisit the file in daylight, with company.

The tradition I come from would say: the part of you that noticed the thought, and read this far looking for something, is also you. That one is not confused about your worth. Keep that one on shift tonight.

> **Honesty note.** *This entry is deliberately not three-layer.* Nothing here is tradition or research or my experience standing in for medical care; it is signposting. Helpline numbers are public service facts, subject to change; the directory is the durable one. If you are immediately at risk of harming yourself, contact emergency services where you are, now.

### 25. How to pick a therapist (and what to beware)

If entry 23 moved you toward the door, here is what I know about walking through it, as someone who trained in this field, refers people constantly, and has sat on both sides of the couch.

Credentials first, glamour second. Wherever you live, the floor is the same: a recognized clinical license; clinical psychology, psychiatry, licensed counseling, or the local equivalent. In China, the rough floor is a hospital psychiatric or clinical-psychology department, or a licensed counselor with real training; be aware that "psychological counselor" became a loose label after a national certification exam was discontinued, so ask about training hours and supervision, not just the title. A helpful rule from the world's regulated systems, worth borrowing anywhere: diagnosis and prescription belong to medical doctors, and structured therapy belongs to people with real training; meanwhile the private market will happily sell you "healing," "energy work," and "spiritual counseling" at hourly rates, none of which is this, whatever their brochures imply.

Then the only evidence that fully matters, checked in the first two or three sessions: do you feel *safe and not judged*, and does the work have a direction? Therapy that is all warmth and no road is a pleasant rental; therapy that is all road and no warmth is an interrogation (see entry 22 for what those do to tired minds). You are entitled to ask a therapist what method they use and how they will know it is working; a good one will enjoy the question. And if the fit is wrong (you feel unseen, managed, or slowly recruited into dependence), you are allowed to leave. Firing a therapist is not a failure of therapy; it is the skill working.

What therapy is not: someone handing you your life, fixed. The best sessions I have witnessed all ended the same way: with the person holding more of themselves than when they walked in, and the door left open. That is the product. Everything else in this book, you can do alone; that one, you do with a witness. It costs money. It is still the cheapest thing on this page for the people who need it.

> **Honesty note.** *The tradition:* the trust-the-process-and-verify framing is consistent with how this school trains practitioners; the regulatory details above are public facts about China's system, stated as I understand them and subject to local variation; check the current rules where you are. *The research:* the warmth-plus-structure point reflects the common-factors literature in broad strokes, not a single citation; treat it as practitioner's counsel. *My own experience:* the single best predictor I have watched, across years of referrals, is not the school of therapy. It is whether the person can say "I don't know" in the first session without flinching.
