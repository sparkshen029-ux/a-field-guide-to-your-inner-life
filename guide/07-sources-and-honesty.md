# Sources and Honesty

[← Back to README](../README.md) · [Next →](../README.md)

**On lineage.** This guide is an exposition, in English, of ideas from the imagery dialogue (*yixiang duìhuà*) tradition founded by Zhu Jianjun (朱建军), professor of psychology, author of the Chinese-language works on which Parts I–IV draw: *Image Dialogue Psychotherapy* (意象对话心理治疗), *Interpreting Dreams* (释梦, revised edition), *How Many Souls Do You Have?* (你有几个灵魂), and *Who Am I: Reading the Self through Imagery* (我是谁). These books have not, at this writing, been translated into English. All renderings, paraphrases, and translations here are mine, with my own errors; the book's voice is mine, and the tradition's ideas are cited to it as carefully as a popular guide allows. Where I have extended, illustrated, or guessed, the Honesty notes say so line by line.

**On research.** Entries cite, by name and with pointers, work and reviews including: Zadra & Stickgold on the NEXTUP model of dreaming; van der Helm & Walker on REM sleep and emotional processing; Domhoff's reviews of dream continuity and the dream-lag effect (with Blagrove and colleagues); Freud's *Interpretation of Dreams* and Jung's compensation thesis (via the IAAP literature); Masten on resilience (via Bartol's criminal psychology textbook); Redondo & Andrés-Pueyo's review of offender treatment; Gudjonsson's handbook on interrogation psychology (sleep, stress, and suggestibility); Balban et al. 2023 on cyclic sighing (*Cell Reports Medicine*, [doi:10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895)); Noetel et al. 2024 on exercise for depression (*BMJ*, [doi:10.1136/bmj-2023-075847](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)); Holt-Lunstad et al. 2015 on social connection (*Perspectives on Psychological Science*, [doi:10.1177/1745691614568352](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352)); and, via *HowToLiveBetter* (see below), [Hoge et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.3679) on mindfulness versus escitalopram, the prolonged-grief trials (Lundorff, Prigerson, Shear), and the grief-intervention reviews (Currier, Wittouck, Bonanno). For nightmares, Rolling and colleagues' 2026 trial of imagery rehearsal therapy in youth with complex PTSD ([doi:10.1007/s44470-026-00098-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44470-026-00098-4)) is also cited in entry 12. Entries link named studies straight to their DOI in the Honesty note wherever a stable target exists (all entries from 26 on, and several earlier); where no link is present, the name and this page are the pointer. New in this revision: studies behind entries 26–39 — materialism and well-being (Dittmar et al. 2014), perfectionism (Smith et al. 2018), rumination (Nolen-Hoeksema et al. 2008), the stressor literature (Selye's general adaptation syndrome; Gudjonsson's handbook), green space (Rojas-Rueda et al. 2019), coffee and sleep (Poole et al. 2017; Drake et al. 2013; Gardiner et al. 2023), implementation intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006), sunk cost (Arkes & Blumer 1985; Roth et al. 2015), couple therapy and marital quality (Roddy et al. 2020; Robles et al. 2014; Kiecolt-Glaser et al. 2005), parenting (Gershoff & Grogan-Kaylor 2016; Heilmann et al. 2021; Wang & Kenny 2014; Furlong et al. 2012; Backhaus et al. 2023), and WHO psychological first aid (2011) — carry their citations inline. Where a claim is craft knowledge or my own observation, the Honesty note says so rather than borrowing a citation it cannot support.

**Companion and debts.** The genre of the honest, graded, sources-or-silence guide belongs to *HowToLiveBetter* (github.com/eternity4719/HowToLiveBetter), a Chinese-language open work under CC BY 4.0; this guide borrows its spirit, several research leads, and its best habit: telling you the author has done about a tenth of it.
